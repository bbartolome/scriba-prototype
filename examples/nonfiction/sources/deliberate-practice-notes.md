# Notes on deliberate practice

Reading notes for chapter 2. Paraphrase only; the wording is mine.

The source is the same 1993 article by K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe and Clemens Tesch-Römer, _The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance_, published in _Psychological Review_.

The article's account sets deliberate practice against playing through what you can already do. Playing through means repeating a skill you have already reached, at the level you already work at.

Deliberate practice, as the article describes it, has these features:

- a well-defined task, set at the right level of difficulty;
- informative feedback, so the learner can see what went wrong;
- chances to repeat the task and correct the errors that the feedback shows.

For chapter 2, the contrast matters more than any measure. These notes give no numbers for it, and the draft should not add any.

These notes cover only the account of deliberate practice. The Berlin violin study and its hours are in the ten-thousand-hour notes, and the telegraph studies are in the plateau notes.
