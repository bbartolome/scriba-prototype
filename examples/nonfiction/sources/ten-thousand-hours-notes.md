# Notes on the ten-thousand-hour idea

Reading notes for chapter 3. Paraphrase only; the wording is mine.

The idea traces to a 1993 article by K. Anders Ericsson, Ralf Krampe and Clemens Tesch-Römer. The article is titled _The Role of Deliberate Practice in the Acquisition of Expert Performance_ and appeared in _Psychological Review_.

The study compared violin students at a music academy in Berlin, grouped by their level. The best group had practised the most over their lives. Their average was about 10,000 hours of practice by age twenty.

That figure is an average, not a threshold. Individuals varied within each group.

A 2008 popular book, Gladwell's _Outliers_, turned the figure into a rule of thumb. Read the study itself before taking the rule.

The study has limits that belong next to the number. It involved few students at one school. Its practice hours were estimated from memory.

When the ten thousand hours appear in the book, the limits appear with them.
