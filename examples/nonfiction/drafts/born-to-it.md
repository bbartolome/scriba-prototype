# Chapter 1: Born to It?

Some people seem built for a thing. A child picks up a melody from a single hearing, or a student sees the shape of a proof before anyone explains it. We call them natural, and the word does a lot of work. It explains the gap between them and the rest of us, and it lets the rest of us stop asking what we might do differently.

The label is comfortable because it is partly true. People do differ, and some of those differences show up early. But the word hides more than it shows. It treats the early lead as the whole story, and it quietly assumes that what came before the lead, the hours and attempts nobody saw, was not worth counting.

Think about the last time someone called you talented. What did they mean? Often they meant that you had made something look easy, and that they had not seen the struggle behind it. The label names the result and forgets the process that produced it.

This book takes the other route. It does not deny that people start in different places. It asks what happens after the start, and what an adult who did not begin as a prodigy can do with the years still ahead.

Some questions will come back in every chapter. What does practice look like when it works? And how do you tell whether you are actually getting better, as opposed to just getting more comfortable? The second question is harder than it sounds, because comfort and progress can feel alike from the inside.

The rest of this chapter sets up the harder question. The next chapter takes it up directly, by separating the kind of practice that changes a person from the kind that mostly repeats what they already do.
