# The storm

The glass fell all afternoon. By dusk the wind had come round to the west and the sea was throwing itself at the skerry hard enough to shake the cups on their hooks.

Tomas went up the tower without a word. Ada followed him, two steps behind, the way she had learned to.

The lamp was still burning when they reached the room, but the flame had begun to lean. A gust came through the seam of the glass and it dropped to a blue bead, then flared, then dropped again.

"Mantle," Tomas said. He was already reaching for the spare.

"I can do it." Ada heard her own voice come out too fast. She made herself slow down. "Tell me what you want me to do, and I will do exactly that."

For once she did not fill the silence. Tomas looked at her, then at the lamp, and then he moved his hand to the cabinet door and left it there. "Left one," he said. "Turn it till you feel the stop. Don't force it."

She turned it. The flame steadied. Tomas breathed out.

Below them the sea went on, and the light swept out over it, and somewhere out beyond the reef a boat that was not coming would not be coming until the spring tide, which was eleven days away.
