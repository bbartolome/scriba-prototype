6. **Automatic gate, fallback and lazy loads (Opus rejection 2, items 2 and 4).** `withDeviceGate(routes)` returns the array with the gate and the fallback added by the rules below, applied at any depth of the arrays it sees:
   - (i) A route with `data.scope` gets `canMatch: [deviceClassGuard(min)]` and its fallback (decision 8). `min` is `data.minDeviceClass`, and an absent value means `tablet` (SHL-01 plan decision 10).
   - (ii) A route whose `canMatch` already holds a `deviceClassGuard`, such as a feature's child `canvas` gated by `deviceClassGuard('tablet')`, gets only the fallback. It keeps its own gate, so no second gate is added.
   - (iii) Every other route is left alone, including untagged children such as the `''` child of `phone-view`. A phone visit of `/phone-view` therefore shows that child (test G8), not the page.
   - (iv) A route with `loadChildren` is wrapped: its loader becomes `async () => withDeviceGate(await original())`, so the loaded `Routes` array gets rules (i) to (iii) as well. A lazily loaded child gate is served without the feature doing anything: GRR-09's `graphs` loader is wrapped by the Shell, and its `:graphId` child gets the fallback. The loader must resolve to a `Routes` array, as SHL-01 decision 9 already requires.
   The `**` entry has no `scope`, so it is never gated. Source: SHL-05 unit lines 19 and 22; SHL-01 plan decision 10; GRR-09 unit lines 15 and 16 (`graphs`, then the lazily loaded `:graphId` child gated by `deviceClassGuard('tablet')`). Decision: the Shell applies the gate, so a feature cannot forget it, and the lazily loaded routes are included.

8. **Generated fallback (Navigator decision, DECISIONS 1852df7; Opus rejection 2, item 3).** For each route that rule (i) or (ii) of decision 6 applies to, `withDeviceGate` adds one sibling immediately after it, in the same array. The sibling copies `path` and `pathMatch` from its route, and has `canMatch: [deviceClassBelow(min)]`, which is `true` only when the device class is below `min`, so exactly one of the pair matches at any navigation. The sibling has no `data` and no `loadChildren`, so the nav never lists it and the gate never wraps it again. Its shape depends on the route:
   - A leaf route (no `children` and no `loadChildren`): the sibling is `{ path, pathMatch, canMatch, component: LargerScreenPage }`.
   - A route with `children` or `loadChildren`: the sibling is componentless, `{ path, pathMatch, canMatch, children: [{ path: '**', component: LargerScreenPage }] }`, so the `**` child takes the URLs under that path.
   The two shapes are needed because a route with no children does not match leftover URL segments. A childless route is matched only when nothing is left over: `processSegment` with an empty child list accepts only an empty remainder (`_router-chunk.mjs` line 3156, which calls `noLeftoversInUrl` at line 3032), and the child processing of a matched route is at lines 3224 to 3240. A `**` child consumes the leftovers. The resulting behaviour, stated in full:
   - (1) On a phone, the page shows at the gated URL for both shapes, including the exact URL of a route with children (the `**` child matches an empty remainder).
   - (2) On a phone, a deep URL under a gated route with children (for example `/deep/x/y`, test G6) shows the page. On a tablet the feature's own children decide that URL.
   - (3) A deep URL under a gated leaf (for example `/tablet-view/x`) is NotFound on a phone and on a tablet alike, because a leaf takes no leftovers.
   - (4) An unknown top-level URL keeps the `**` NotFound page on every device (test G7), because a fallback copies a gated path and matches nothing else (SHL-01 plan decision 8).
   The generated siblings are not written into the route table; they exist only in the arrays that `withDeviceGate` returns (section 2). Source: DECISIONS 1852df7 (the constraint paragraph); SHL-01 plan decision 8. Decision.
